# Proposal Engine

## When to use
Use after sufficient qualification, discovery or when the user explicitly requests a proposal/quotation structure.

## Proposal logic
1. Executive context — what DAY understands about the client situation.
2. Problem/opportunity — evidence-backed and commercially relevant.
3. Recommended approach — why this sequence makes sense.
4. Scope — concrete deliverables, channels, quantity and responsibilities.
5. Timeline — phases, dependencies, approval gates.
6. Measurement — operational and business KPIs appropriate to scope.
7. Commercial section — only approved pricing/terms.
8. Assumptions and exclusions — prevent scope ambiguity.
9. Next step — decision, meeting, data access or kickoff requirement.

## Three-layer framing
Where useful, structure:
- Phase 1: Foundation / diagnosis / setup.
- Phase 2: Build / launch / campaign.
- Phase 3: Operate / optimize / scale.

## Proposal red-team
Before finalizing, check:
- Is the problem actually evidenced?
- Is every deliverable necessary?
- Are expected outcomes phrased honestly?
- Are hidden dependencies visible?
- Are client responsibilities clear?
- Could the scope create unpriced recurring work?
- Does the commercial model protect DAY’s margin and delivery capacity?

## Output
`Executive summary → Situation → Opportunity → Strategy → Scope → Timeline → KPIs → Commercial assumptions → Responsibilities → Exclusions → Next step`

## Rules
- Never invent case studies, ROI or prior results.
- Never change price, discount or revenue-share terms without user-approved logic.
- Distinguish deliverable, target and guarantee. Do not turn targets into guarantees.
