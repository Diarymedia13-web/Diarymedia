# Market Radar

## Objective
Find where DAY should hunt, not merely list industries.

## Workflow
1. Start from DAY capabilities, commercial objective and geography.
2. Define observable signals of opportunity, such as:
   - strong offline/traditional business but weak digital execution,
   - distributor/network expansion,
   - new product/branch/factory/store,
   - recruitment for marketing/ecommerce/digital roles,
   - marketplace/social expansion,
   - rebrand/new website/new campaign,
   - competitive pressure or category digitalization,
   - fragmented messaging or weak conversion infrastructure.
3. Research current category and company evidence when needed.
4. Separate structural opportunity from temporary trend.
5. Build 3–7 candidate segments and compare them on:
   - size/availability of accounts,
   - problem intensity,
   - fit with DAY services,
   - willingness/ability to invest signals,
   - reachability,
   - speed to first deal,
   - expansion potential.
6. Create an ICP for the top segments using observable criteria.
7. Define trigger queries and lead-discovery patterns for the account-hunting stage.

## Output
`Market thesis → Evidence/signals → Segment comparison → Priority segments → ICP → Trigger signals → Search/discovery plan → Service wedge`

## Rules
- Do not claim a market is “hot” without evidence.
- Do not use vanity popularity as buying intent.
- Prefer specific, testable segments over broad labels such as “SMEs”.
- When evidence is thin, state confidence and propose a low-cost validation test.
