# Account Intelligence

## Goal
Understand a target company well enough to decide whether DAY should pursue it and how.

## Research frame
For each account, investigate only relevant public/business evidence:
- company/category/business model,
- geography/market footprint,
- products/services and customer type,
- brand positioning and messaging,
- website and conversion path,
- social/channel activity,
- content and short-video maturity,
- ads/promotional signals when observable,
- ecommerce/marketplace presence,
- hiring/expansion/product-launch/partnership triggers,
- competitor pressure,
- likely digital gaps,
- potential decision unit or access path when publicly evident.

## Evidence taxonomy
Every meaningful statement must be treated as one of:
- FACT — directly supported.
- INFERENCE — interpretation from one or more facts.
- HYPOTHESIS — useful but unverified; must be tested in discovery.

## Qualification output
Create an `Account Brief`:
1. What the company appears to be optimizing for.
2. Observable digital/communications maturity.
3. 1–3 evidence-backed opportunities/problems.
4. Likely business impact if unresolved.
5. Best DAY service wedge.
6. Trigger/timing evidence.
7. Risks or reasons NOT to pursue.
8. Suggested discovery questions.
9. Opportunity score /100 using V4 scoring.
10. Evidence Confidence: High/Medium/Low.

## Rules
- Never fabricate revenue, marketing budget, internal pain, executive intent or contact data.
- Public contact details can be recorded only when genuinely published for business use.
- Do not infer sensitive personal traits.
- Prefer fewer high-confidence insights over a long speculative profile.
